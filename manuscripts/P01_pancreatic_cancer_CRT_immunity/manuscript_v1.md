## Introduction

<!-- [2026-10-18] 日本語案 materials/(参考)Introduction_日本語案.md(第2版)を英語化。数値・記述の出典ページは materials/(参考)Introduction_事実と数値の抜粋.md を参照。引用タグは references/refs.yaml に登録済み。 -->

Pancreatic cancer remains one of the most lethal malignancies, and despite advances in multimodal treatment, long-term survival is achieved by only a small proportion of patients {Siegel_39817679}. Histologically, pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) consists of relatively few neoplastic cells embedded in an abundant fibrotic stroma with a poorly developed vasculature {Halbrook_37059070; Stoop_40187844}. This stroma not only impedes the delivery of anticancer agents but also creates an immunosuppressive microenvironment that limits the entry of cytotoxic T cells into the tumor {Kabacaoglu_30158932; Balachandran_30660727}.

For patients with resectable disease, surgical resection combined with systemic chemotherapy is the standard treatment; nevertheless, recurrence, largely attributable to occult micrometastases, develops in the majority of patients {Springfeld_36932224; Mannucci_40971231}. Neoadjuvant chemoradiotherapy (NACRT) has been investigated as one approach to overcome this limitation. In the phase III PREOPANC trial, which enrolled patients with resectable or borderline resectable pancreatic cancer, gemcitabine-based NACRT improved overall survival, with a 5-year overall survival rate of 20.5% compared with 6.5% after upfront surgery {Versteijne_35084987}. However, median survival differed by only 1.4 months, and the role of neoadjuvant treatment has not been firmly established {Versteijne_35084987; Springfeld_36932224}. Better outcomes for patients with pancreatic cancer therefore remain an unmet need.

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have changed the treatment of many cancers. In the PACIFIC trial, which included patients with unresectable stage III non-small-cell lung cancer, consolidation therapy with durvalumab, an anti-programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) antibody, after chemoradiotherapy prolonged progression-free survival {Antonia_28885881}, and the 5-year overall survival rate was 42.9%, compared with 33.4% in the placebo group {Spigel_35108059}. In pancreatic cancer, by contrast, clinical trials of ICIs given without radiotherapy have produced disappointing results. Ipilimumab, an anti-cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen 4 (CTLA-4) antibody, given as a single agent produced no objective responses {Royal_20842054}; the combination of durvalumab and tremelimumab yielded an objective response rate of only 3.1% {OReilly_31318392}; and nivolumab, alone or with ipilimumab, induced no responses {Callahan_38316517}.

New therapeutic strategies are therefore needed for immunotherapy to benefit patients with pancreatic cancer. Radiotherapy not only damages tumor cells directly but can also induce antitumor immune responses. We have previously shown that CD8+ T cells contribute to the therapeutic effect of irradiation in mouse tumor models {Yoshimoto_24686897}, that chemoradiotherapy induces tumor antigen-specific T-cell responses in patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma {Suzuki_22700877}, and that preoperative treatment increases the expression of human leukocyte antigen (HLA) class I on tumor cells in rectal cancer and esophageal squamous cell carcinoma {Sato_25259797; Takehara_37772585}. HLA class I on the surface of tumor cells is indispensable for the recognition of tumor antigens by cytotoxic T cells, and its reduction or loss is one of the mechanisms by which tumors escape antitumor immunity {Hiraoka_32495519}. PD-L1 expressed by tumor cells binds to programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) on T cells, suppresses T-cell function, and is the target of anti-PD-1 and anti-PD-L1 antibodies {Kabacaoglu_30158932}. In pancreatic cancer, an increase in tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells after NACRT has also been reported {Homma_NACRTimmuneimpact_24310792; Gartrell_35558160}. However, these studies were small and differed in their chemotherapy regimens and radiation doses. Furthermore, the findings on HLA class I have been inconsistent {Homma_NACRTimmuneimpact_24310792; Michelakos_32497200}, and few studies have assessed CD8+ T cells, HLA class I, and PD-L1 together. Determining whether radiotherapy activates the immune microenvironment of pancreatic cancer is essential for evaluating the potential of immunotherapy in this disease.

In the present study, we used immunohistochemistry to evaluate tumor-infiltrating CD8+ T cells, HLA class I, and PD-L1 in pancreatic cancer specimens resected after gemcitabine-based NACRT and in those resected without preoperative treatment, in order to examine whether NACRT activates tumor immunity in pancreatic cancer.

### Treatment

Patients in the NACRT group received chemoradiotherapy with single-agent gemcitabine. Radiotherapy was delivered concurrently with gemcitabine in conventional fractionation to a total dose of 54 Gy in 30 fractions, encompassing the primary tumor with a margin and the regional lymph node area. Patients in the comparator group underwent radical resection without any preceding chemotherapy or radiotherapy.

### Immunohistochemical staining

Formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded surgical specimens were cut into 4-μm-thick sections, deparaffinized in xylene, and rehydrated through a graded ethanol series. Antigen retrieval conditions were optimized separately for each marker. For CD8, sections were heated in Target Retrieval Solution, pH 9 (S2367; Agilent Technologies, Santa Clara, CA, USA) at 100°C for 20 minutes. For HLA class I, sections were autoclaved in Target Retrieval Solution Citrate, pH 6 (S2369; Agilent Technologies) at 121°C for 10 minutes and allowed to cool at room temperature for 1 hour. For PD-L1, sections were autoclaved in Target Retrieval Solution, pH 9 (S2367; Agilent Technologies) at 120°C for 10 minutes. After retrieval, sections were rinsed in deionized water and washed in phosphate-buffered saline (PBS). Endogenous peroxidase activity was blocked with Dako REAL Peroxidase-Blocking Solution (S2023; Agilent Technologies) for 15 minutes.

Sections were then incubated overnight at 4°C for 16 hours with the following primary antibodies: anti-CD8 (1:600; clone C8/144B; Agilent Technologies), anti-HLA class I-ABC (1:400; clone EMR8-5; Hokudo, Sapporo, Japan), and anti-PD-L1 (1:400; clone E1L3N; Cell Signaling Technology, Danvers, MA, USA). For CD8 and HLA class I, an avidin-biotin-complex-labeled anti-mouse secondary antibody (VECTASTAIN ABC-HRP Kit; Vector Laboratories) was applied for 30 minutes at room temperature. For PD-L1, a horseradish-peroxidase-conjugated anti-rabbit polymer (Envision+ System-HRP; Agilent Technologies) was applied for 30 minutes at room temperature. Immunoreactivity was visualized with 3,3′-diaminobenzidine, and all sections were counterstained with hematoxylin.

### Evaluation of immunohistochemical staining

Immunohistochemical staining was evaluated based on the methods used in our institution's previous study of the tumor immune microenvironment {Takehara_37772585}. For assessment of CD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, hotspot areas of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes were selected in four independent fields within the intratumoral region and the invasive front of each surgically resected specimen at ×200 magnification. CD8+ cells in each field were counted using Patholoscope version 1.8.0 (MITANI Co., Fukui, Japan), and the mean count across the four fields was calculated. HLA class I expression was scored according to the percentage of tumor cells showing positive membranous staining, using a five-tier system: 0, <1%; 1, 1–9%; 2, 10–49%; 3, 50–79%; and 4, ≥80%. Specimens were considered PD-L1-positive when dark brown membranous staining was observed in >1% of tumor cells. CD8 and HLA class I staining were each assessed independently by two authors (K.U. and T.S.), and discrepant results were resolved by joint reassessment and consensus.

### Statistical analysis

Continuous and ordinal variables, including the number of CD8+ cells and the HLA class I expression score, were analyzed using the Mann-Whitney U test; results are reported as medians with 95% confidence intervals estimated by bootstrap resampling (10,000 iterations). Categorical variables, including patient background characteristics, PD-L1 positivity, and the dichotomized HLA class I expression (score 0–2 versus 3–4), were analyzed using Fisher's exact test. A two-sided p-value of <0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using Python version 3.9.21 with the pandas (version 2.2.3), SciPy (version 1.13.1), and NumPy (version 1.26.4) libraries.
